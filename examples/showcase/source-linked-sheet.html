What this page is claiming, and what it is not — including why the sheet slides off

One four-dimensional triangular sheet is the model. It stretches, folds, and settles against a separate finite obstacle — a connected block of tetrahedra, drawn as the triangles that actually bound it — and everything below is a view of it. Selecting in either canvas names the source triangle that produced the pixel, and says plainly whether that pixel determines a point in R4 at all.

The sheet slides off the obstacle, and this page does not hide it. Gravity points along W; the obstacle is a finite block only about 0.9 deep in Z. The sheet is pinned at two corners and spreads sideways as it settles, so within the first few dozen steps its lowest vertices are already outside the block's footprint in Z. Nothing supports them there, and by roughly step 150 the sheet has dropped below the obstacle's plane and is swinging on its pins. It goes past the edge of the support, not through its face — the barrier is doing what a finite contact source can do, and the obstacle is simply far smaller than the excursion. Play the replay to watch it. The right-hand view cannot show this at all, because leaving the footprint happens in Z and that view drops Z — which is this page's own point, arriving uninvited.

Discrete cosine-fold bending — not a shell model. The fold stiffness here is an unsigned, mesh-dependent discrete term whose energy is quartic in the fold angle. It does not converge to a continuum bending energy under refinement, and its stiffness is not a transferable material constant. This page is a demonstration of source-retained geometry, mechanics, representation, and selection — not a cloth or shell simulator.

Simulate advances the solver, and every step costs a full pass over each provider — that is why a finer sheet is a different scene and restarts. The search switch picks an equivalent way to organise the broadphase: same ordered candidates, same trajectory, same result. It is not an accelerator — on this compact obstacle the tree tests every bound anyway. Replay costs nothing: applied steps are recorded as they are solved, and playing them back re-shows the configuration the solver produced together with the numbers it produced alongside it. Nothing is re-solved, and the inspector's showing row always names which step you are looking at.
